Simple: the app asks little and shows the day

Round 3 was correct but it was a banking app: tabs, a feed, forms, settings. Simple keeps every trust rule and replaces the interface with what people already handle: banknotes, pictures, voice, and one short evening ritual where the app does the filing and the person says "c'est juste".

Grounding: in Microsoft Research studies with low-literacy users in India, Kenya, the Philippines and South Africa, text interfaces were unusable for first-time users, graphical interfaces had the best completion for mobile banking, and spoken dialog was fastest for those comfortable with it (Medhi et al., ToCHI). Voice feedback and a consistent help feature mattered (Medhi 2007), and menu hierarchies stayed hard even without text (CHI 2013).

  1. Ask little, show the day. The app files what it can guess and asks only the rest. Each evening ends with "C'est juste ?".
  2. Numbers and pictures, words last. One-colour pictograms (a custom set to draw), banknotes, counts with their unit.
  3. Every screen can speak. The same 🔊 in the same place; voice answers inside the ritual.
  4. Flat beats deep. Two places, Moi and Nous, and one evening ritual.
  5. Say only what is true today. Today's spending, not a forecast. "J'apprends encore" until there's evidence.
  6. Private by default, never hidden by gesture. Amounts behind a visible eye; the ledger behind a visible row.
  7. Approximate and daily beats exact and abandoned. One bag for tonight's cash is enough.

Decisions this direction needs from you

  1. Adopt Simple as the Phase 1 direction (Fable recommends it), with round 3 kept as the source of the trust screens.
  2. Pocket amounts hidden by default, shown with the eye. This replaces the onboarding question you chose on 7 October.
  3. Account timing: Clerk username + password at first open (today), or created later, silently, when a njangi needs an identity.
  4. Pictograms: commission one 20-icon, one-colour set (Material Symbols stand in here). Emoji only in Phase 0 paper tests.

Test in Phase 0 interviews, not by opinion: the "today's spending" headline vs a "ça tient jusqu'au" date; banknotes vs keypad; which pictures people name correctly; whether Android's "envoyer" wording scares people.
