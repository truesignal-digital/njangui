Every franc, every pocket. A money app built for how Cameroon pays.

Cash in the wallet, Orange Money on one SIM, MoMo on the other, savings locked in a njangi, money owed by a cousin. Nobody sees the whole picture today. This draft explains how we can, what the operators will and won't give us, and how far the idea can go.

Built on research from Orange Developer, MTN MoMo Developer, BEAC, the World Bank, Google Play policy and the Cameroon competitors that already exist. Sources are at the end. Claims we could not confirm are marked.

The short answer

Your question was: can we ask Orange Money for a member's latest transactions when they connect, read-only, with no transfers? Not through any public API today. But we can still get most of that data, and we can get it this year.

Official API: no, not yet

Orange Money Cameroon APIs (Web Pay, CoreAPIs) only serve merchants: start a payment, check its status. MTN MoMo's Open API has a user-consent flow (bc-authorize → GetUserInfoWithConsent), but it returns identity, not history. GetAccountBalance reads our merchant wallet, not the user's. No aggregator (CamPay, Notch Pay, CinetPay, pawaPay…) sells consumer history either.

The data path: SMS, plus import

Every Orange Money and MoMo transaction sends an SMS with the amount, the other party, a transaction ID, and usually the new balance. Google Play explicitly allows READ_SMS for "SMS-based money management: apps that track and manage budget". On Android (about 83% of Cameroon's mobile web traffic) we can capture transactions automatically. On iOS we use share, paste, screenshot and statement import.

The catch: SMS alone is no moat

MoniTrack (claims 50,000+ active users) and WalletEye already parse OM/MoMo SMS in Cameroon. So reading SMS is table stakes. What sets us apart is everything an SMS can't see: cash, njangis, family obligations, debts between people, and the two-sided confirmation we already built. That is where the app wins.

The direction in one sentence: expand OnCotise from "the njangi ledger" into the money record of a Cameroonian household. Capture Mobile Money automatically, make cash quick to log, and treat njangis, family support and personal debts as real money with real positions. Never move the money ourselves, at least at first.

The problem: money lives in five places, and none of them talk

A typical salaried worker in Douala gets paid into a bank or MoMo, cashes out part of it, pays the moto driver in cash and the boutique via Orange Money merchant code, sends 15,000 F to Maman, pays 20,000 F into the office njangi and lends 10,000 F to a colleague. At month end they have no idea where 60,000 F went.

The operators' apps each show one wallet, and only about 10 recent lines in the MoMo app. The USSD mini-statement shows 5 lines (#150#, *126*7*2#). Nobody shows cash. Nobody treats a njangi as savings. Nobody tracks the 10,000 F lent to the colleague.

What an SMS gives us, and the trick that makes it trustworthy

Operator SMS usually carry the amount, counterparty name and number, fee, transaction ID and balance after. The balance is the key. Each new SMS must equal the previous balance plus or minus the transaction. If it doesn't, we know an SMS is missing (deleted, phone off, number changed). We can ask the user to fill exactly that gap instead of guessing. This "balance chain" fits the house rule of server truth over optimism: the ledger proves its own completeness.

SMS is sensitive. Google's rule and Law 2024/017 point the same way. Parse on the phone. Send only structured transaction fields to Convex, never raw SMS. Never read or upload messages from anyone other than known operator sender IDs. This also gives us a true and simple privacy promise: "we read only Orange, MTN and your bank, and only on your phone."

The product: pockets, not accounts

Cameroonians don't think in "accounts". They think in pockets: the money on Orange, the money on MTN, the cash in hand, the njangi, what Paul owes me. The app models exactly that.

Mobile Money pockets, automatic

One pocket per SIM per operator; many people run two or three. SMS fills them on Android, share/paste/OCR on iOS. Transfers between your own wallets count as moves, not spending.

Why it matters: 55% of adults have MoMo/OM, and dual-SIM is normal. Double-counting own transfers is the #1 way trackers lie.

Cash pocket, anchored by cash-outs

Every "Retrait 20 000 F" SMS adds cash to the cash pocket. Then a nightly 10-second check: "how much cash do you have left?" The difference is spent; tap 1–3 categories or say it by voice. No one has to log every beignet.

Why it matters: cash is the blind spot of every tracker. Cash-out SMS give us the inflow for free; we only ask for the outflow.

Njangi as a real position

Before you take the pot, your njangi is savings: +120,000 F after six rounds. After you take it, it's an interest-free loan you repay each round. We already hold this data in the ledger. Show it as money.

Why it matters: 38% of adults save this way and no app counts it in net worth. This is ours alone.

Debts & family, two-sided

"I lent Brice 10,000 F." Brice gets a link and confirms with one tap, the same handshake as our PaymentRecord. Repayments match automatically when a MoMo SMS from Brice's number arrives.

Why it matters: informal lending is huge and runs on memory. We already built the confirm/dispute machinery.

Envelopes that match Cameroonian life

Default envelopes: Loyer, Ration, Transport, Crédit & data, Famille, Njangi & réunions, Église / association, École, Santé, Imprévus (deuils, fêtes). Budgets planned per month and per season.

Why it matters: imported budget apps think in "Dining out" and "Netflix". Here the real lines are family support and funerals.

The fee and tax lens

Every SMS shows the fee. We total them: "This month: 6,412 F in fees and tax. 4,800 F came from cash-outs you could have avoided by paying the merchant code directly."

Why it matters: cash-out costs about 1%, transfers carry 0.2% + 4 F. The total stays invisible until someone adds it up, and the advice pays for the app.

Going further: where this can grow

You asked for ambition. These are the expansions that grow from what we're building, not side bets. Each one reuses the ledger, the two-sided confirmation, or the transaction stream.

Anti-scam shield

Fake "Vous avez reçu 50 000 F" SMS from a normal phone number, followed by "please send it back", is a classic Cameroon scam. We already read operator SMS, so we know the real sender IDs. We flag the fake one and check it against the wallet balance.

Hook: a reason to install that has nothing to do with budgeting, and it fits Google's anti-smishing goals.

Diaspora earmarks

A brother in Lyon sends 150,000 F "for school fees". He gets a read-only view where the recipient attaches receipts and marks the money used. Both sides confirm. It is a Contribution group with one sponsor.

Hook: $690M a year flows in, with constant "where did my money go?" friction. The diaspora pays in euros and can afford a subscription.

Household mode

A couple or family shares envelopes (loyer, ration, école) while keeping personal pockets private. Each person's SMS feed contributes only what they choose to share.

Hook: household budgeting is the real unit of money in Cameroon. Sharing also brings in a second user per household.

Buyam-sellam mode

For small traders: split business from household money, log daily sales with voice, see what they really earn, and track customer credit ("ardoise").

Hook: 86.6% informal employment. Kippa, Bumpa and Biashara show trader bookkeeping sells in Nigeria and Kenya.

Financial passport

A report the user owns: income regularity, njangi contributions confirmed on time, debts repaid. They choose to share it with a microfinance lender as a PDF or link. Consent-based, user-held, revocable.

Hook: njangi reliability is real creditworthiness that no bank can see. This becomes our partner revenue later.

Talk to your money

"Combien j'ai dépensé en transport cette semaine?" "How much moto this week?" A voice and text assistant in French, English and Pidgin over the structured ledger. It answers from data and never guesses amounts.

Hook: low literacy and reluctance to type are real barriers. An LLM over a clean ledger is cheap now; the clean ledger is the hard part, and we'd have it.

WhatsApp companion

Njangi reminders, "you owe Brice" nudges, a weekly summary and a quick "log 2000 taxi" by message. Useful for iOS users and feature-phone family members.

Hook: money conversations already start in WhatsApp. Our contribution links already spread there.

Seasonal planner

The year has known spikes: rentrée (August–September), Christmas and New Year, Ramadan and Tabaski, funerals that arrive without warning. The app turns each into a goal with a monthly target, and can suggest a njangi to fund it.

Hook: people already use njangis to time big expenses. We just make the plan explicit.

Partner products, later

Once we hold trust and data, licensed partners (an EMF, a bank, a microinsurer, Orange or MTN themselves) can offer savings, insurance and credit in the app. We earn referral fees; they hold the money and the licence.

Hook: how Chumz, Chamasoft and PiggyVest's partner model make money, without us becoming a regulated custodian.

Architecture: parse on the phone, sync structure

This fits the current stack (Expo, Convex, Clerk) with one native addition: an Android SMS module. That needs a small Expo config plugin and a native module. We'd build it in-house, because the SMS permission has to be justified by our own core feature.

Rules and trust

What we can do without a licence

Reading your own SMS, recording cash and tracking njangis does not take deposits, extend credit or execute payments. Those are the activities BEAC Règlement 04/18 regulates. So a read-only tracker very likely needs no COBAC or e-money licence. That is our inference, not an official exemption. Get a short written opinion from a Cameroonian fintech lawyer before launch.

The line moves the moment we move money (MoniTrack does, through transfers), hold funds, or lend. Payment institutions need XAF 500 M of paid-up capital. We stay non-custodial and partner with licensed institutions for anything that touches funds.

Data protection: Law No. 2024/017

Cameroon's personal data protection law was promulgated on 23 December 2024 and fully in force from 23 June 2026, after an 18-month grace period. It creates a data protection authority. Practical duties for us: explicit consent, purpose limitation, data minimization, user rights (access, correction, deletion), security, and breach notification. Financial SMS are about the most sensitive data a person has, so the design above (parse on the phone, upload structure only) is not optional.

Trust promises we can actually keep

  • We never move your money. Same as OnCotise's current line: "Your Mobile Money stays yours."
  • We read only Orange, MTN and bank messages, on your phone. Sender allowlist, enforced in code.
  • No ads, no data selling. The financial passport is shared only by you, one lender at a time, and can be revoked.
  • Delete everything from the profile in one step. The law requires it anyway.

Roadmap

Strangler, not big-bang, just like our code rule. The njangi pilot stays live; personal finance lands beside it as a new tab. Every phase ends at a gate where you decide whether to continue.

Phase 0 · ~3 weeks

Prove the data

  • Collect 300–500 real OM/MoMo/bank SMS from consenting pilot members (anonymized) → parser fixtures
  • 15 interviews: salaried, trader, student, diaspora sender
  • Email Orange (business.orangemoney@orange.com) and MTN Cameroon to ask about consented read access
  • Lawyer opinion: read-only status + Law 2024/017 checklist
  • Dry-run Play SMS declaration on an internal track
Gate: parser handles ≥95% of fixtures; ≥10 of 15 interviewees want the cash check or njangi position.
Phase 1 · ~6–8 weeks

Voir (see)

  • Android SMS capture + backfill, iOS share/paste + OCR
  • Pockets home, feed, balance-chain gaps
  • Cash check (tap first, voice later)
  • Default categories + fee lens
  • Scam-SMS flag
  • Njangi position on the home screen
Gate: ≥80% of OM/MoMo transactions captured automatically on Android; 4-week retention beats the current pilot.
Phase 2 · ~8 weeks

Planifier (plan)

  • Envelopes, monthly plan, seasonal goals (rentrée first)
  • Two-sided debts with SMS matching
  • Household sharing
  • Plus + Group Pro pricing test
  • WhatsApp reminders
Gate: some users pay; debts get confirmed by the other side.
Phase 3–4 · 2027

Ensemble & partenaires

  • Diaspora earmarks
  • Assistant (FR/EN/Pidgin)
  • Buyam-sellam mode
  • Financial passport + first EMF partner
  • Operator API if a deal or the CEMAC regime lands
Gate: partner signed, regulator view in writing.
Timeline assumes the current 2-dev team plus agent help, and that the six pilot blockers from the 25 September review are fixed first. Personal finance should not start on a base whose CTAs are invisible on Android.
