Feature Status · custody-free njangi ledger

The committed backend is far ahead of the frontend: the custody-free engine — cycle lock + locked rotation order, the full two-sided PaymentRecord handshake (claim/confirm/dispute/cancel/resolveDispute), round lifecycle internals, and the 15-min lifecycle cron — is built and deployed, but no UI calls any of it (zero api.cycles/rounds/paymentRecords references across app/ and src/). The frontend is roughly at end-of-week-1: phone auth, name onboarding, groups list, the group-creation wizard, invites/join, the member roster, approvals, and feature-phone add are wired end-to-end. Net: the trust ledger exists server-side but is not yet usable by members; the next build phase is purely UI, starting with the cycle-start screen and the round/claim flow.

Screen exists and is wired to the backend. A member can do this now.

Phone (SMS OTP) sign-in / sign-up

Sign in or create an account by phone number via Clerk SMS OTP; a Convex user row is provisioned on first login (app/(auth)/sign-in.tsx -> clerk-auth-screen.tsx, api.users.current + getOrCreateCurrentUser).

Why The phone number is the identity members already recognize and the single point of failure for in-person onboarding — it's what lets a champion sign 15-30 members up live at the reunion.

Name onboarding

A first-time or name-less user sets the display name the group will call them by, then routes home (app/(app)/onboarding.tsx, api.users.updateMyName, gated by needsOnboarding redirect).

Why The feed and roster name people, never IDs, so a recognizable display name is required before a member can appear in the group's trust artifacts.

FR/EN language toggle

Switch the app between French and English; the choice persists locally and syncs to the user record when signed in (language-toggle.tsx, api.users.updateLanguage, i18n in src/lib/i18n.ts).

Why Pilot groups and their summaries/notifications are bilingual FR/EN, so language must follow the member for the WhatsApp share and notification copy to land.

My groups home list

A signed-in member sees their njangi groups with member count, amount, schedule and status, and taps into each (app/(app)/index.tsx -> group-card.tsx, api.groups.listMyGroups).

Why The group is the unit of the product; the home list is how a member who belongs to multiple njangis reaches the one in session.

Group creation wizard

A 4-step wizard (name -> rhythm + fixed amount -> money-flow mode + creator role -> invite) persisted by one createGroup mutation (app/(app)/groups/new.tsx, api.groups.createGroup -> groupId + inviteCode).

Why Onboarding whole existing njangis through a champion is the entire acquisition funnel, and a fixed amount + schedule is the locked trust property the engine later enforces.

Invite code + WhatsApp share

A president/treasurer copies the 6-char code or shares an invite message to WhatsApp to recruit members (invite-share.tsx in groups/new.tsx step 4 and group detail).

Why WhatsApp invite codes are the distribution loop that markets the app inside groups with no web page, borrowing WhatsApp's strength instead of fighting it.

Join group by invite code

A user pastes an invite link/message or types a code to join, landing in pending-approval (or active if pre-added by phone) (app/(app)/join-by-code.tsx, api.memberships.joinViaCode).

Why Njangis are closed trust circles that must not auto-admit, so code + approval is the safe entry path; reusing the same phone reattaches all memberships.

Group detail / member roster

A member views the roster with roles, account/no-app and status badges, plus the group's amount, rhythm and collection-mode rules (group detail -> member-list.tsx, api.groups.getGroup + api.memberships.listMembers).

Why The roster is the shared source of truth for who is in the circle and in what role — the base layer the rotation order and ledger are built on.

Pending-member approval queue

A president/treasurer approves or rejects pending join requests from the group detail screen (member-list.tsx, api.memberships.approveMember / rejectMember, gated by canManage).

Why Because njangis are closed trust circles, president/treasurer approval is the gate that keeps the group a real trust circle rather than an open chat.

Feature-phone member add

A president/treasurer adds a smartphone-less member by name + Cameroon phone (auto-normalized to E.164), creating an active membership with no account (add-member.tsx, api.memberships.addFeaturePhoneMember).

Why A large share of real njangi members (the market/bayam-sellam segment) have no smartphone; logging them keeps the whole group on the app instead of the notebook, and they earn reliability scores normally.

Backend ready — engine built, needs a UI

Committed & deployed to Convex (the Week 2 slice), but no screen calls it yet, so members can't use it. This is the trust ledger — it exists server-side but is invisible until the UI lands.

startCycle (cycle lock)

President-only mutation that locks rotation order and materializes the cycle — snapshots amount/schedule onto a cycles row and bulk-generates one round per position with computed open/due/grace timestamps (convex/cycles.ts:80). No frontend caller.

Why The locked, publicly-visible, immutable rotation order is the tamper-evident trust property offline njangis already have but can't prove.

getActiveCycle (public rotation order)

Query returning the active cycle's locked rotation order — beneficiary names, per-round status, current round id, or null when no cycle runs (convex/cycles.ts:435). No useQuery caller.

Why Locked-and-public means no silent edits; exposing the order is how the rotation becomes a visible trust artifact rather than a private notebook.

recordOrderChange (swap / remove)

President-only mutation for post-lock edits with a mandatory note — swaps two future beneficiaries, or removes an exited/deceased member's round and shifts later rounds one period earlier, writing an immutable orderChanges row (convex/cycles.ts:283). No frontend caller.

Why Supports the routine 'begging the turn' swap practice without reverting to the notebook, while keeping every change immutable and attributed.

Round lifecycle internals (openRound / closeRound)

Internal mutations driving scheduled->open (pre-creates pending contribution + payout PaymentRecords) and grace->closed (freezes obligation statuses, flags arrears, advances to payout/completed), invoked only by the cron (convex/rounds.ts:482/492).

Why Pre-creating pending records at round open is what powers the Meeting Mode roll-call and the treasurer-entry kill-signal metric.

getRound (round detail / pot progress)

Query returning a round's status, beneficiary, expected vs confirmed vs in-flight pot totals, payout status and frozen obligation statuses (convex/rounds.ts:573). No useQuery caller.

Why This is the data behind the member's round card; without UI reading it, pot progress stays invisible to the group.

listRoundPayments (roll-call rows)

Query returning per-member contribution rows — status chip, confirmed-vs-expected, isSettled, individual records — to power the round card and Meeting Mode roll-call (convex/rounds.ts:653). No frontend caller.

Why These rows are the roll-call the treasurer ticks through at the reunion; they are the backbone of the killer cash feature.

PaymentRecord handshake - claim

Mutation where payer (j'ai envoye) or payee (Meeting Mode tick) asserts a payment with idempotency key, method and optional proof; self-records go pending->confirmed and shortfalls split into a fresh pending record (convex/paymentRecords.ts:443). No caller.

Why The claim is half of the custody-free core: the app legally cannot touch money, so a payer/payee assertion is the only primitive available.

PaymentRecord handshake - confirm

Mutation where the non-claiming side acknowledges a claim (truth = payee confirmation); president may confirm for an unrepresented counterparty with a mandatory note, advancing payouts and flipping fines to paid (convex/paymentRecords.ts:611). No caller.

Why Payee confirmation is the only truth — it kills the 'j'ai paye / tu n'as pas paye' palabre that WhatsApp and the notebook can't resolve.

PaymentRecord handshake - dispute

Mutation where the counterparty contests a claim (Je n'ai pas recu) with a mandatory reason, opening a feed-visible litige and a single open disputes row (convex/paymentRecords.ts:720). No caller.

Why Disputes terminate the handshake instead of letting claims rot, and the dispute rate is itself a kill-signal that the confirmation UX is misdesigned.

PaymentRecord handshake - cancel (withdraw)

Mutation where a claimant withdraws their own claim/declaration (claimed or disputed -> cancelled), re-creating a fresh pending record when the obligation is still unmet (convex/paymentRecords.ts:804). No caller.

Why A claimant must be able to walk back a mistaken declaration so the ledger records reality rather than a stuck false claim.

PaymentRecord handshake - resolveDispute

Mutation closing a dispute via payee late-confirm, payer withdrawal, or president override (mandatory >=10-char note + immutable presidentOverrides row), moving record and dispute in one transaction (convex/paymentRecords.ts:896). No caller.

Why The president can always 'Trancher' via an immutable override, so disputes can always terminate rather than poisoning the group.

Lifecycle cron tick (15-min)

Internal mutation run every 15 minutes that opens/grace/closes rounds at their timestamps and applies claimed-record timers — reminders, auto-confirm, anchored auto-dispute — across the deployment (convex/crons.ts:43, scheduled every 15 min).

Why Payee-side cash entries auto-confirm on silence and payer claims auto-flag at 72h, so feature-phone entries never rot and the handshake always reaches a terminal state.

reassignTreasurer

President-only mutation that moves the single treasurer role to another active member and demotes the previous treasurer to member (convex/memberships.ts:329). Zero frontend callers, unlike its sibling membership functions.

Why The treasurer is the buyer and treasurer-churn is a named risk (R2); a president-reassignable role mitigates it without rebuilding the group.

Meeting Mode (treasurer roll-call)

L

Treasurer-only roll-call that batch-creates claimed records (20 entries in 2 min) with an AsyncStorage offline tap-queue. No batch-claim backend entrypoint and no UI; only the single-record claim exists.

Why The reunion roll-call is the killer cash feature that beats the notebook at its own job; the tap queue prevents silent loss when Android kills the backgrounded app.

Member exit / deceased handling

President mutation + UI to mark a member exited or deceased, freezing the round and dropping/skipping future rounds via a 'remove' OrderChange and driving the two payout branches. Status values and orderChangeKind 'remove' exist, but no mutation or caller; no UI.

Why Real njangis lose members mid-cycle; without this the rotation can't be safely repaired and the engine's remove path stays unreachable.
